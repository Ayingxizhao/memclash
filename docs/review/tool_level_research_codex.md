## 1. What other systems do

The central lesson is that syntax checks and semantic checks are different. Your tool schema can guarantee a well-formed translation, but it cannot guarantee that the translation means the same thing as the rule.

| Technique | What it does and evidence | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Decompose before formalizing** | `nl2spec` links each formal subformula to the source-language fragment it represents. On 36 difficult requirements, initial accuracy was 44.4%; interactive correction of sub-translations reached 86.1%, averaging 1.4 correction loops. [nl2spec paper](https://www.react.uni-saarland.de/publications/CHMST23.pdf) ARTEMIS goes further: translate into a structured natural-language intermediate form, then deterministically compile it. Across five requirements datasets it reported 1.57× the accuracy of the strongest baseline. [ARTEMIS paper](https://cs.stanford.edu/~trippel/pubs/mendoza_ICSE26.pdf) | Usually two stages/calls instead of one; more intermediate data to validate. |
| **Use a modular or pattern-based target** | Lang2LTL separates entity grounding from temporal-logic translation; NL2LTL supports a fixed library of temporal patterns. These resemble your fixed vocabulary and make decisions smaller. [Lang2LTL](https://lang2ltl.github.io/) and [NL2LTL](https://francescofuggitti.github.io/papers/2023/aaai2023fc.pdf) — **I inspected only their abstracts/demo descriptions.** | No necessary extra calls, but requires designing an intermediate representation or candidate library. |
| **Constrained decoding and grammars** | Grammar-constrained decoding guarantees syntactic membership in the output language. [Geng et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.674.pdf) GraFT also restricts choices during grounding and translation, reporting average gains of 5.49 points end-to-end and 14.06 points out-of-domain. [GraFT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/english25a.html) | Little or no extra model inference, but decoder integration work. It cannot by itself detect a missing requirement. Your tool schema already obtains most of its structural benefit. |
| **Generate several answers and vote or rank** | Self-consistency samples independent reasoning paths and votes; it improved several reasoning benchmarks by 3.9–17.9 points. [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171) LINC found that majority voting helped its formal-logic pipeline, especially when weaker models produced invalid formulas. [LINC](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.313.pdf) Text-to-SQL systems also vote by execution result rather than textual equality. [SQLPrompt](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.39/) — **abstract only for SQLPrompt.** | Roughly \(k\) translations per rule. Voting only helps if errors are not systematic and equivalent outputs are canonicalized first. |
| **Execute or solve, then repair** | Text-to-SQL execution-guided decoding rejects candidates that fail during execution and reached 83.8% execution accuracy on WikiSQL. [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03100) — **abstract only.** Logic-LM uses solver errors to repair formalizations and reported gains of 39.2% over ordinary prompting and 18.4% over chain-of-thought. [Logic-LM](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.248/) — **abstract only.** SatLM and SymbolicAI use the same broad solver-plus-model pattern. [SatLM](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/8e9c7d4a48bdac81a58f983a64aaf42b-Abstract-Conference.html), [SymbolicAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.00854) — **abstracts only.** | Solver checks are cheap; repair costs another call. Crucially, they prove correctness only relative to the parsed specification. LINC explicitly warns that lost information can still mislead the solver. |
| **Back-translation or round trips** | Translate formal → English → formal and compare the two formal forms. A recent study on 150 traffic rules reported that diagnosis-guided round-trip repair raised formal equivalence from 45–61% to 83–85%. [Amrollahi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25031) — **abstract only.** Cycle consistency has also been used to filter semantic-parser training data. [GAZP](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.558.pdf) | Usually two extra model calls plus a cheap equivalence check. A consistently wrong initial interpretation can still survive. |
| **Policy extraction into fixed slots** | Access-control work extracts subject, object, action and relation fields rather than free-form policy code. A NIST-associated study found poor zero-shot performance and much better few-shot results, but warns that its near-perfect scores likely reflect LLM-synthesized data similar to its examples. [Lawal et al.](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=958734) | Often one call; strong dependence on realistic evaluation data. |

## 2. Fit to this pipeline and the four failures

Here F1 is omission, F2 is “concrete called vague,” F3 is needless ambiguity, and F4 is paraphrase inconsistency.

| Candidate pipeline change | Failures addressed | Cost per rule | Measurement |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Atomic requirement inventory**: Stage A returns a list of requirements, each tied to an exact source span and classified as representable, unmodeled, or genuinely vague. Stage B translates every representable item; code rejects any inventory item without a clause or explicit exclusion. | Strong for F1–F2; helps F3 and F4 by making each decision smaller. | 2 calls total if all atoms are translated together. | Requirement-level precision/recall; percentage of source requirements represented; whole-rule exact semantic accuracy. |
| **Independent semantic coverage gate**: deterministically render clauses into simple canonical English, then give the source, inventory, and rendering to a checker model. It labels each atom covered, contradicted, unsupported, or missing. Reject and retry on failure. Prefer a different model or at least a separate context. | F1–F3 directly; catches spurious `files_shown` in F4. | +1 checker call; +1 repair call only when rejected. | On deliberately mutated translations, checker precision/recall for dropped and added fields; then end-to-end accuracy after repair. |
| **Plain back-translation** | F1–F3 if the comparison is effective. | +1 call for formal → English; +1 if re-formalizing. | Detection rate on semantic mutations. |
| **Three-to-five translations plus voting**: canonicalize clause order and defaults before comparing. | Mainly F4; sometimes F1/F3. Weak for systematic F2. | 3–5 translation calls. Your three existing measurement runs could be reused. | Accuracy versus number of samples; paraphrase agreement; abstention rate when no majority exists. |
| **Generate candidates, then select**: retain distinct valid translations from several samples or enumerate a small set of field alternatives; an independent ranker chooses against the atomic inventory. Solver-equivalent candidates are merged first. | F3–F4 and stochastic F1. | 3 translations plus 1 ranker, or one call returning several candidates plus 1 ranker. | Oracle pass@3, selected top-1 accuracy, and gap between them. |
| **Second-model checker without an inventory** | Potentially all four, but the judgment is difficult to audit. | +1 call. | Checker precision/recall and whether its errors correlate with the translator’s. |
| **Z3 feedback loop** | Formal contradictions, impossible fields, or equivalent candidates—not F1–F3. | Almost free unless repair is invoked. | Invalid-form reduction and downstream clash accuracy. Do not count it as semantic coverage evidence. |
| **More grammar constraints** | Layout and vocabulary errors only. | Decoder engineering, no extra call. | Schema validity; unlikely to move the four semantic failures. |

For evaluation, freeze a blind, human-annotated set before implementation. Include compound rules, lists, context-disambiguated words, and paraphrase families. Report atomic requirement recall, whole-rule exact accuracy, `ok`/`unclear`/`not_in_scope` macro-F1, paraphrase agreement, downstream clash precision/recall, calls, tokens, and latency. Do not score only clause similarity: a translation missing one conjunct should fail the whole-rule metric.

## 3. Best options for the Dec. 1 presentation

1. **Implement the atomic inventory followed by per-atom translation.** This is the clearest, most defensible change and closely follows `nl2spec` and ARTEMIS. It converts “remember every nuance” into a testable pipeline invariant: every identified requirement must be translated or explicitly excluded. Show improved atomic recall and whole-rule accuracy on a frozen sample. It will **not** fix an atom that was extracted correctly but mapped to the wrong vocabulary field.

2. **Add an independent coverage gate with mutation testing.** Create incorrect variants automatically by deleting clauses, removing conditions, adding fields such as `files_shown`, or changing `ok` to `unclear`. Measure whether the checker detects them, then measure whether reject-and-repair improves end-to-end accuracy. This directly targets all four pilot failures. It will **not** provide a formal guarantee, and a checker sharing the translator’s bias may accept the same mistake.

3. **Reuse the three existing runs as candidates and rank them, rather than treating agreement only as a report statistic.** Canonicalize first, discard schema-invalid candidates, and let the coverage checker choose; abstain if none covers every atom. Measure top-1 accuracy, oracle pass@3, and paraphrase agreement. It will **not** help when all samples make the same systematic mistake, and it triples translation cost outside the existing evaluation runs.

- Structured output solves form, not meaning.
- Decomposition plus source-span traceability is the strongest near-term defense against omissions.
- An independent coverage gate is more relevant than additional Z3 feedback for these four failures.
- Multi-sample selection improves instability but cannot cure correlated errors.
- Success should mean complete semantic translation on a frozen blind set, plus better downstream clash recall—not merely valid JSON or higher agreement.
