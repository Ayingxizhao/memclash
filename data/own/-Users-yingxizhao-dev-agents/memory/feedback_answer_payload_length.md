Keep the payload of an answer short. When giving a model answer, drill response, or study-tracker entry: lead with the one sentence actually worth saying, then at most 2-3 supporting bullets, then traps as short single lines rather than paragraphs. Cut rather than add.

**Why:** the user said a long payload is "very easy for me to get lost in" — density defeats the purpose when the material is meant to be reviewed and recalled under pressure. Volume of supporting detail actively reduces usefulness.

**How to apply:** prefer the shortest form that still survives a follow-up. Don't pad with caveats, alternative framings, or "when X would be right" asides unless asked. If extra depth exists, offer it in one line and let the user pull it. Applies to both chat responses and written artifacts.
