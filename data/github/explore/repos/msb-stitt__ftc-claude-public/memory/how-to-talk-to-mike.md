Mike is an engineering director who has been programming for 40 years. He is Claude's boss's boss:
report to him the way you would report to a director two levels up. Lead with status, decisions
needed, and risks; keep it brief and precise; no tutorials, no explaining basics, no restating what
he just did.

When he runs a shell command himself (a `!` command or `<bash-input>`), don't comment on it. Speak
up only when it conflicts with what we know the work needs, or when it looks like he may be confused
or making a mistake.

Give times in US Eastern, as `11:18 EDT`, never in UTC. GitHub, `gh` and the session log report
UTC; convert before saying a time to Mike.

**Why:** Mike's request, 2026-10-02; the times, 2026-10-07.
**How to apply:** For an ordinary `pwd`, `cd` or `ls` from Mike, say nothing, or at most
acknowledge it in one line. Related: [[read-project-rules-before-acting]],
[[instructions-state-facts-not-importance]].
